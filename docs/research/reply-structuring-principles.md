# Reply-structuring principles: source research

Research date: 2026-08-23

## Bottom line

The strongest common rule is not simply “be brief.” It is **put the reader's highest-value unit first, then disclose supporting detail in decreasing order of need**. Depending on the request, that first unit is an answer, decision, result, warning, or next action. BLUF, GOV.UK, both agent skills, and several executive-summary guides all support this structure; ASD-STE100 reinforces it at sentence and paragraph level with one topic, explicit sequence, and gradual disclosure. [U.S. Army BLUF lesson](https://www.7atc.army.mil/Media-News/Video/dvpTag/writing/dvpmoduleid/4969/videoid/1016589/) [GOV.UK content-design principles](https://guidance.publishing.service.gov.uk/writing-to-gov-uk-standards/plan-manage-content/understand-content-design/) [i-have-adhd source](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ayghri/i-have-adhd/main/skills/i-have-adhd/SKILL.md) [Caveman source](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/JuliusBrussee/caveman/main/skills/caveman/SKILL.md) [ASD-STE100 Issue 9](https://www.asd-ste100.org/assets/files/ASD-STE100_ISSUE9.pdf) [OECD Style Guide](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/corrigenda/OECD-Style-Guide-Third-Edition.pdf)

The most important design warning is equally clear: **do not equate compression with damaged grammar**. Caveman's full mode drops articles and permits fragments, but ASD-STE100 says not to omit words merely to shorten a sentence and says to use an article or demonstrative adjective where applicable. GOV.UK calls for language that is “brisk, but not terse.” For an intuitive human-editing style, Caveman's removal of filler is useful; its routine article deletion is not. [Caveman rules](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/JuliusBrussee/caveman/main/skills/caveman/SKILL.md) [ASD-STE100 Issue 9, rules 4.2 and 4.5](https://www.asd-ste100.org/assets/files/ASD-STE100_ISSUE9.pdf) [GOV.UK tone](https://guidance.publishing.service.gov.uk/writing-to-gov-uk-standards/tone-of-voice/right-tone/)

## Source quality and scope

- **BLUF:** U.S. military first-party sources. The Army University Press lesson defines the operational pattern: main point in the opening paragraph and first line of each section. The official Air Force handbook warns that excessive stage-setting can hide the bottom line. [Army University Press lesson](https://www.7atc.army.mil/Media-News/Video/dvpTag/writing/dvpmoduleid/4969/videoid/1016589/) [Air Force Handbook 33-337](https://static.e-publishing.af.mil/production/1/administrative_assistant/publication/afh33-337/afh33-337.pdf)
- **ASD-STE100:** the current official standard is Issue 9, dated 15 January 2025. It is a controlled natural language for technical documentation, not a general correspondence style. Its own FAQ says its basic principles can be adopted more widely. [Official home](https://www.asd-ste100.org/index.html) [Official FAQ](https://www.asd-ste100.org/faq.html) [Issue 9 PDF](https://www.asd-ste100.org/assets/files/ASD-STE100_ISSUE9.pdf)
- **Caveman:** the original source is JuliusBrussee/caveman; the canonical skill file is `skills/caveman/SKILL.md`. [Original repository](https://github.com/JuliusBrussee/caveman) [Raw skill](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/JuliusBrussee/caveman/main/skills/caveman/SKILL.md)
- **i-have-adhd:** the relevant original source is ayghri/i-have-adhd, not the similarly named iizcm mirror/packaging repository. Its README describes the skill as loosely based on *The Adult ADHD Tool Kit* but adapted for assistant output; the rules should therefore be treated as a product design, not as a clinical standard. [Original repository](https://github.com/ayghri/i-have-adhd) [Raw skill](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ayghri/i-have-adhd/main/skills/i-have-adhd/SKILL.md) [README and attribution](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ayghri/i-have-adhd/main/README.md)
- **GOV.UK:** current first-party Government Digital Service content guidance, supplemented by the official research background. [Writing standards](https://guidance.publishing.service.gov.uk/writing-to-gov-uk-standards/) [Research background](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/govuk-content-principles-conventions-and-research-background/govuk-content-principles-conventions-and-research-background)
- **Executive summaries:** there is no single universal style. Official guides vary with report purpose. The OECD starts with takeaways and then labels findings and recommendations; U.K. research guidance includes aim, brief method, findings, conclusions, and recommendations; the European Court of Auditors emphasizes conclusions and recommendations while minimizing descriptive material; GAO uses modular sections and explicitly leaves placement to judgment. [OECD guide](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/corrigenda/OECD-Style-Guide-Third-Edition.pdf) [U.K. research-report guide](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/government/uploads/system/uploads/attachment_data/file/466003/Research_Publication_-_Formatting_and_Style_guide.pdf) [European Court of Auditors](https://methodology.eca.europa.eu/aware/PA/Pages/Reporting/Executive-summary.aspx) [GAO guide](https://www.gao.gov/products/130024)

## Highest-impact principles by approach

### BLUF

1. **Lead with the critical point.** Put it in the opening paragraph and repeat the same discipline at the first line of each section. [Army University Press](https://www.7atc.army.mil/Media-News/Video/dvpTag/writing/dvpmoduleid/4969/videoid/1016589/)
2. **Use one or two opening sentences, then amplify.** An official Air Force commentary describes BLUF as a one- or two-sentence critical point followed by details in the body. [Creech Air Force Base](https://www.creech.af.mil/News/Commentaries/Article/2578011/techniques-for-feedback-up-the-chain-of-command/)
3. **Move requested action early.** Army guidance says the most important sentence and paragraph should come first, and required action often outranks contextual information. [U.S. Army Acquisition Support Center](https://asc.army.mil/web/news-alt-amj17-technically-speaking-youre-saying-what/)
4. **Treat setup as optional.** Too much scene-setting can obscure purpose and confuse the reader. [Air Force Handbook 33-337](https://static.e-publishing.af.mil/production/1/administrative_assistant/publication/afh33-337/afh33-337.pdf)

Reply implication: first line should be independently useful. “Bottom line” is semantic, not a required label.

### ASD-STE100

1. **Use one stable term for one meaning.** Approved dictionary words generally have one approved meaning and one part of speech; rule 9.4 requires consistent terminology or wording for the same action. [Official overview](https://www.asd-ste100.org/about_STE.html) [Issue 9, sections 1 and 9](https://www.asd-ste100.org/assets/files/ASD-STE100_ISSUE9.pdf)
2. **Write short, structurally complete sentences.** Procedures have a 20-word maximum; descriptive sentences have a 25-word maximum. The standard separately says not to omit words merely to shorten sentences. [Issue 9, rules 4.2, 5.1, and 6.3](https://www.asd-ste100.org/assets/files/ASD-STE100_ISSUE9.pdf)
3. **Keep one instruction per sentence and show order explicitly.** Multiple actions share one sentence only when they happen simultaneously; work steps normally use numbers or letters. [Issue 9, rule 5.2](https://www.asd-ste100.org/assets/files/ASD-STE100_ISSUE9.pdf)
4. **Prefer active voice and command form for actions.** Passive voice is permitted in descriptive writing when the agent is unknown. [Issue 9, rules 3.6 and 5.3](https://www.asd-ste100.org/assets/files/ASD-STE100_ISSUE9.pdf)
5. **Give information gradually.** Each descriptive sentence should have one subject; each paragraph should have one topic, begin with a topic sentence, and contain no more than six sentences. [Issue 9, rules 6.1 and 6.4–6.6](https://www.asd-ste100.org/assets/files/ASD-STE100_ISSUE9.pdf)
6. **Use vertical lists for complex text.** This converts dense syntax into visible structure. [Issue 9, rule 4.3](https://www.asd-ste100.org/assets/files/ASD-STE100_ISSUE9.pdf)
7. **State safety information as condition/command plus consequence.** Safety instructions identify risk level, start with a clear command or condition, and explain the possible result. [Issue 9, rules 7.1–7.3](https://www.asd-ste100.org/assets/files/ASD-STE100_ISSUE9.pdf)

Reply implication: borrow STE's ambiguity controls and terminology discipline. Do not claim strict STE compliance unless the controlled dictionary and all 53 rules are actually checked; the official site warns that plausible AI text is not verified compliance. [Official downloads and AI warning](https://www.asd-ste100.org/STE_downloads.html)

### Caveman

1. **Delete non-informational language.** The skill removes filler, pleasantries, empty hedging, redundant narration, decorative elements, and long raw logs. [Caveman source, rules](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/JuliusBrussee/caveman/main/skills/caveman/SKILL.md)
2. **Preserve technical payload exactly.** It keeps technical terms, code, error text, negation, numbers, and units; it rejects invented abbreviations that increase decoding effort. [Caveman source, rules 16–19](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/JuliusBrussee/caveman/main/skills/caveman/SKILL.md)
3. **Use compressed fact-action-reason order.** Its pattern is `[thing] [action] [reason]. [next step].` [Caveman source, pattern](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/JuliusBrussee/caveman/main/skills/caveman/SKILL.md)
4. **Let clarity override compression.** The skill restores normal language for security, irreversible confirmations, ordered multi-step work, ambiguity, and clarification. [Caveman source, Auto-Clarity](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/JuliusBrussee/caveman/main/skills/caveman/SKILL.md)

Reply implication: keep its information-density test—every deletion must preserve meaning—but prefer its `lite` rules over routine article deletion or fragment grammar.

### i-have-adhd

1. **Lead with the next action.** If the answer is a command, path, or snippet, it goes first. [i-have-adhd source, rule 1](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ayghri/i-have-adhd/main/skills/i-have-adhd/SKILL.md)
2. **Make sequences externally visible.** Number multi-step tasks, keep one bounded action per step, use the fewest steps that work, and restate current state across turns. [i-have-adhd source, rules 2 and 5](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ayghri/i-have-adhd/main/skills/i-have-adhd/SKILL.md)
3. **Suppress competing branches.** Finish the current issue before surfacing another; ask for unresolved user input once, at the end. [i-have-adhd source, rule 4](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ayghri/i-have-adhd/main/skills/i-have-adhd/SKILL.md)
4. **Show concrete progress and errors.** State what now works; for errors, name location, cause, and fix without emotional framing. [i-have-adhd source, rules 7 and 8](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ayghri/i-have-adhd/main/skills/i-have-adhd/SKILL.md)
5. **End with one small next action when work remains.** The skill specifies one action doable in under two minutes and removes generic closers. [i-have-adhd source, rules 3 and 10](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ayghri/i-have-adhd/main/skills/i-have-adhd/SKILL.md)
6. **Rank and chunk.** It caps a list at five items, splitting larger sets into “do now/later” or “must/nice to have.” [i-have-adhd source, rule 9](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ayghri/i-have-adhd/main/skills/i-have-adhd/SKILL.md)
7. **Keep meaningful uncertainty.** Its pre-send check removes empty hedging but explicitly retains hedges that carry real uncertainty. [i-have-adhd source, pre-send check](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ayghri/i-have-adhd/main/skills/i-have-adhd/SKILL.md)

Reply implication: generalize “action first” to “highest-value unit first.” A factual question needs the answer first; an irreversible operation needs the warning first; an actionable task needs the next action first.

### GOV.UK writing style

1. **Start with the user need.** GOV.UK content is designed around what users need to know or do, and may be reduced, split, reformatted, or removed to serve that need. [GOV.UK content design](https://guidance.publishing.service.gov.uk/writing-to-gov-uk-standards/plan-manage-content/understand-content-design/)
2. **Use plain, specific language.** Prefer common short words, avoid jargon and vague organizational language, and explain necessary specialist terms on first use. [GOV.UK clear language](https://guidance.publishing.service.gov.uk/writing-to-gov-uk-standards/writing-guidelines/clear-language/)
3. **Prefer active, user-centered sentences.** Passive voice is acceptable when the outcome matters more than the actor or when it is more user-centered. [GOV.UK clear language](https://guidance.publishing.service.gov.uk/writing-to-gov-uk-standards/writing-guidelines/clear-language/)
4. **Keep chunks short, not artificially tiny.** Current guidance recommends splitting sentences over 25 words and limiting paragraphs to five sentences; it explicitly says overall length should follow what good writing requires. [GOV.UK clear language](https://guidance.publishing.service.gov.uk/writing-to-gov-uk-standards/writing-guidelines/clear-language/)
5. **Use scan anchors.** Clear, descriptive titles use audience language; bullets make text easier to read; numbered steps guide a process; the most useful terms are front-loaded in titles and links. [GOV.UK titles](https://guidance.publishing.service.gov.uk/writing-to-gov-uk-standards/writing-guidelines/clear-titles/) [GOV.UK A-to-Z guide](https://guidance.publishing.service.gov.uk/writing-to-gov-uk-standards/style-guides/a-to-z-style-guide/)
6. **Be brisk but human.** The specified tone is clear, concise, specific, informative, and “brisk, but not terse”; friendliness must not dilute precision. [GOV.UK tone](https://guidance.publishing.service.gov.uk/writing-to-gov-uk-standards/tone-of-voice/right-tone/)

Reply implication: make headings and bullets carry meaning on their own. Formatting should support navigation, not decoration.

### Executive-summary styles

Official guidance supports at least three useful variants; this taxonomy is a synthesis, not a named universal standard.

1. **Decision/takeaway-first:** start with main takeaways, then clearly label key findings and recommendations. This best fits recommendation, options, and status replies. [OECD Style Guide](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/corrigenda/OECD-Style-Guide-Third-Edition.pdf)
2. **Analytical/report-summary:** state aim, a brief method, all material findings and conclusions, and evidence-linked recommendations. This best fits research, diagnosis, and audit replies. [U.K. research-report guidance](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/government/uploads/system/uploads/attachment_data/file/466003/Research_Publication_-_Formatting_and_Style_guide.pdf) [U.K. National Screening Committee checklist](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/uk-nsc-evidence-review-process/reporting-checklist-for-uk-nsc-evidence-summaries)
3. **Conclusion-and-recommendation emphasis:** minimize descriptive setup; summarize what was done, found, concluded, and recommended; retain enough information to stand alone. This best fits executive updates where the full analysis is optional. [European Court of Auditors](https://methodology.eca.europa.eu/aware/PA/Pages/Reporting/Executive-summary.aspx)

Across variants, the durable properties are: self-contained meaning, fidelity to the underlying work, plain language for non-specialists, and a deliberate choice of what the reader needs if they read nothing else. [OECD Style Guide](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/corrigenda/OECD-Style-Guide-Third-Edition.pdf) [European Court of Auditors](https://methodology.eca.europa.eu/aware/PA/Pages/Reporting/Executive-summary.aspx) [GAO guide](https://www.gao.gov/assets/130024.pdf)

## Exact conflicts and recommended resolution

1. **Caveman article deletion vs complete grammar.** Caveman full mode drops `a/an/the` and permits fragments. STE rule 4.2 says not to omit words to shorten sentences, and rule 4.5 requires articles or demonstratives when applicable. GOV.UK also wants brisk language without terseness. **Resolution:** adopt filler deletion, short synonyms, and exact technical payload; keep normal grammar by default. [Caveman](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/JuliusBrussee/caveman/main/skills/caveman/SKILL.md) [ASD-STE100 Issue 9](https://www.asd-ste100.org/assets/files/ASD-STE100_ISSUE9.pdf) [GOV.UK tone](https://guidance.publishing.service.gov.uk/writing-to-gov-uk-standards/tone-of-voice/right-tone/)
2. **Action-first vs answer-first.** i-have-adhd says the first line is an action; BLUF says it is the critical point; executive summaries may require a finding or recommendation. **Resolution:** front-load the reader's highest-value unit, selected by reply type. Use action-first only when action is the answer. [i-have-adhd](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ayghri/i-have-adhd/main/skills/i-have-adhd/SKILL.md) [Army BLUF lesson](https://www.7atc.army.mil/Media-News/Video/dvpTag/writing/dvpmoduleid/4969/videoid/1016589/) [OECD Style Guide](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/corrigenda/OECD-Style-Guide-Third-Edition.pdf)
3. **Delete hedging vs preserve uncertainty.** Caveman broadly drops hedging; i-have-adhd retains hedges that convey real uncertainty; GAO advises qualifiers when needed to avoid misleading readers. **Resolution:** delete social or empty hedging, preserve calibrated confidence, assumptions, and evidence limits. [Caveman](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/JuliusBrussee/caveman/main/skills/caveman/SKILL.md) [i-have-adhd](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ayghri/i-have-adhd/main/skills/i-have-adhd/SKILL.md) [GAO guide](https://www.gao.gov/assets/130024.pdf)
4. **Hard list cap vs exhaustive procedure.** i-have-adhd caps lists at five; STE permits as many explicit work steps as the procedure requires. **Resolution:** cap visible groups, not necessary content—chunk a long sequence into stages without hiding required steps. [i-have-adhd](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ayghri/i-have-adhd/main/skills/i-have-adhd/SKILL.md) [ASD-STE100 Issue 9, rule 5.2](https://www.asd-ste100.org/assets/files/ASD-STE100_ISSUE9.pdf)
5. **No recap vs self-contained state.** i-have-adhd bans redundant post-task recaps but also requires state to be restated across turns; executive summaries are intentionally self-contained summaries. **Resolution:** remove ceremonial recap, retain one outcome/state line when it changes what the reader understands or does next. [i-have-adhd](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ayghri/i-have-adhd/main/skills/i-have-adhd/SKILL.md) [European Court of Auditors](https://methodology.eca.europa.eu/aware/PA/Pages/Reporting/Executive-summary.aspx)
6. **Front-load conclusion vs known-before-new context.** GOV.UK's research background notes that inverted-pyramid order and known-before-new order can conflict, particularly in complex passages. **Resolution:** keep the conclusion first, but attach the minimum qualifier or context required to interpret it correctly. [GOV.UK research background](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/govuk-content-principles-conventions-and-research-background/govuk-content-principles-conventions-and-research-background)
7. **One executive-summary layout vs genre-specific layouts.** OECD recommends labeled findings and recommendations, while the European Court of Auditors says its executive summaries should not have headings; GAO says placement varies with the issue. **Resolution:** treat executive-summary structure as a family of patterns chosen by task, not a fixed reply template. [OECD Style Guide](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/corrigenda/OECD-Style-Guide-Third-Edition.pdf) [European Court of Auditors](https://methodology.eca.europa.eu/aware/PA/Pages/Reporting/Executive-summary.aspx) [GAO guide](https://www.gao.gov/assets/130024.pdf)
8. **Strict sentence limits vs natural prose.** STE's 20/25-word limits apply to controlled technical writing; GOV.UK uses 25 words as a split signal rather than a universal hard ceiling. **Resolution:** use 20–25 words as an editing alarm, not an automatic rejection rule for general replies. [ASD-STE100 Issue 9](https://www.asd-ste100.org/assets/files/ASD-STE100_ISSUE9.pdf) [GOV.UK clear language](https://guidance.publishing.service.gov.uk/writing-to-gov-uk-standards/writing-guidelines/clear-language/) [STE FAQ on general writing](https://www.asd-ste100.org/faq.html)

## Consolidated principles worth carrying into later skill design

The following ordering is an evidence-informed synthesis, not a draft skill:

1. **Front-load value:** answer, result, decision, warning, or action first.
2. **Layer detail:** essential meaning first; evidence, rationale, and optional background later.
3. **One unit, one job:** one topic per paragraph; one bounded action per step; one meaningful claim per sentence where practical.
4. **Use scan anchors:** descriptive headings, short bullets, numbered procedures, and visible state.
5. **Prefer plain precision:** common words for general prose; exact technical terms, identifiers, code, errors, negation, numbers, and units.
6. **Compress semantics, not grammar:** delete filler and duplication; retain connective words needed for sequence, causality, scope, and exceptions.
7. **Close on state:** when work remains, end with one concrete next action; when complete, end with the verified outcome.
8. **Let risk override brevity:** warnings, irreversible actions, real uncertainty, and ambiguous sequence receive enough language to prevent misreading.
9. **Adapt the frame to the reply:** explanation, procedure, recommendation, status, diagnosis, and research summary need different first units and different depth.

Every item above is supported by multiple primary sources cited in the preceding sections. Its ranking reflects relevance to assistant replies and human scan/edit behavior, not a controlled comparative study.

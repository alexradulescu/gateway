# Sydney small apartment block: feasibility, rules and indicative cost

**Research date:** 16 August 2026  
**Question:** Buy an existing Sydney house for A$1 million, demolish it and build 4–6 self-contained three-bedroom homes for friends.

## Short answer

This is possible on the right property, but **not on an arbitrary Sydney house lot**. The address must permit the chosen housing type, and the permitted floor-space ratio (FSR), frontage, parking, landscaping, access and setbacks must fit the unusually large apartment mix. A cheap, narrow R2 lot outside a mapped low-and-mid-rise area may not permit an apartment building at all.

A reasonable first-pass project budget, including the assumed A$1 million land price, purchase costs, demolition, design/approvals, construction, contingency and finance, is:

- **Four 3-bedroom homes in a two-storey manor-house / low-rise form:** about **A$4.5–7.3 million all-in**, or roughly A$1.1–1.8 million per home.
- **Six 3-bedroom apartments in a three-storey boutique block:** about **A$6.1–10.3 million all-in**, or roughly A$1.0–1.7 million per apartment.

These are feasibility allowances, not quotations. A basement, lift, difficult rock, contamination/asbestos, major stormwater or utility upgrades, retaining walls, poor access, premium finishes, or a longer approval/finance period can push the result above the range. Conversely, a simple two-storey design, surface parking and a clean flat site are the best chance of reaching the lower end.

The most plausible low-cost concept is **four dwellings in a two-storey manor-house form** on roughly 625 m² or more with about 15 m frontage. The NSW Pattern Book's [Manor Homes 01](https://www.planning.nsw.gov.au/government-architect-nsw/housing-design/nsw-housing-pattern-book/pattern-designs/manor-homes-01-by-studio-johnston) is a useful official reality check: four 1–3 bedroom apartments, about 83 m² each, on a minimum 625 m² / 15 m-wide site, with 0.60:1 FSR and two parking spaces. Four genuinely generous three-bedroom homes would likely need more area.

## The first decision: what are you actually building?

NSW planning law distinguishes forms that can look similar in everyday language:

| Form | Practical description | Likely route / consequence |
|---|---|---|
| **Multi-dwelling housing** | Three or more dwellings, each with ground-level access; no dwelling is above another | Permitted with consent throughout R1/R3 and, under the low-and-mid-rise reforms, in qualifying R2 land. In qualifying R2, the state non-discretionary standards include 600 m² minimum lot, 12 m width, 0.7:1 FSR, 9.5 m height and one parking space per dwelling. |
| **Manor house** | Three or four attached dwellings, no more than two storeys, with at least one over another | A subset of low-rise housing that may qualify for a complying development certificate (CDC) under the Low Rise Housing Diversity Code if every site and design standard is met. It is the strongest candidate for four homes without a lift or basement. |
| **Residential flat building (RFB)** | Three or more apartments, two or more storeys, with at least one apartment lacking direct ground access | Normally a development application (DA), not ordinary complying development. This is the likely category for six stacked apartments. |

The official [Low Rise Housing Diversity Code](https://www.planningportal.nsw.gov.au/development-and-assessment/codes-sepp/low-rise-housing-diversity-code) explains the manor-house CDC pathway. A proposal that misses even one CDC standard can still be possible, but it must use the DA route and be assessed on its merits.

## Where state reforms help—and where they do not

The NSW [low-and-mid-rise housing policy](https://www.planning.nsw.gov.au/policy-and-legislation/housing/low-and-mid-rise-housing-policy) has applied since 28 February 2025 around nominated stations and town centres. A “low-and-mid-rise housing area” is generally residential-zoned land within 800 m walking distance of a nominated centre or station; it is not every R2 property.

The state's [summary of key provisions](https://www.planning.nsw.gov.au/the-planning-system/housing/low-and-mid-rise-housing-policy/summary-of-key-provisions) gives these important non-discretionary standards:

- On qualifying **R1/R2** land, an RFB can be permissible with consent with a 500 m² minimum lot, 12 m width, 0.8:1 FSR, 9.5 m height and 0.5 parking space per dwelling.
- On **R3/R4 land in the inner 0–400 m area**, the corresponding RFB controls are 2.2:1 FSR, 22 m and up to six storeys.
- In the **outer 400–800 m area**, they are 1.5:1 FSR, 17.5 m and up to four storeys.

“Non-discretionary” does **not** mean automatic approval. It means council cannot refuse merely because the proposal meets that particular standard; all other controls and impacts remain assessable. Local environmental plans (LEPs) and development control plans (DCPs) still matter for such things as setbacks, landscaping, tree retention, privacy, solar access, streetscape, waste, stormwater and heritage. The [NSW guide to the planning system](https://www.planning.nsw.gov.au/for-homeowners/your-guide-to-the-da-process/getting-started/understanding-the-planning-system) explains the LEP/DCP roles.

Separate rules apply inside mapped Transport Oriented Development areas. The state's [TOD guidance](https://www.planning.nsw.gov.au/sites/default/files/2024-05/guidance-to-transport-oriented-development.pdf) describes a typical 22 m height, 2.5:1 FSR, 21 m minimum lot width and a 2% affordable-housing contribution around mapped stations. Always check the current statutory map rather than assuming distance from a station is enough.

The state reforms also have [important exclusions](https://www.planning.nsw.gov.au/the-planning-system/housing/low-and-mid-rise-housing-policy/low-and-mid-rise-housing-policy-exclusions), including specified bushfire, flooding, aircraft-noise, coastal-hazard, pipeline and heritage-item land. Other mapped hazards and a local heritage conservation area can still materially constrain a DA even when they are not a blanket exclusion.

## Why the advertised minimum lot size may still be too small

The Apartment Design Guide (ADG) minimum internal area for a three-bedroom apartment is **90 m²**, plus 5 m² for each bathroom beyond the first. Its other headline criteria include private open space, communal open space, deep-soil planting, solar access, natural cross-ventilation and building separation. See the official [Apartment Design Guide](https://www.planning.nsw.gov.au/sites/default/files/2023-03/apartment-design-guide.pdf).

That creates an immediate yield test:

- Four apartments need at least 360 m² of internal apartment area before walls, stairs, corridors, services or storage. On a 500 m² R1/R2 lot at 0.8:1 FSR, the entire permitted gross floor area is only 400 m². The legal minimum lot is therefore unlikely to support four comfortable three-bedroom apartments.
- Six apartments need at least 540 m² internal before common areas and walls. At 0.8:1 FSR, even 675 m² of land only supplies 540 m² GFA. In practice, a six-by-three-bedroom proposal at that FSR may need roughly **800–1,000 m²**, a useful frontage and an efficient above-ground parking solution.

“Minimal backyard” cannot mean no landscape. On projects governed by the ADG, communal open space is normally 25% of site area and deep soil 7%, subject to the Guide's qualifications. Council tree-canopy, landscape and stormwater controls may add further requirements.

Housing SEPP Chapter 4 generally applies to a new RFB of at least three storeys and four dwellings. It requires consideration of the ADG and design principles and brings architect/design-verification requirements. A two-storey manor house avoids that chapter's apartment-design trigger, but it still must meet its applicable planning code or DA controls, BASIX and the National Construction Code.

## Building-code and construction obligations

- Stacked self-contained apartments will generally be **NCC Class 2**. The [ABCB classification guide](https://ncc.abcb.gov.au/ncc-navigator/building-classifications) should be confirmed by the certifier for the actual design.
- Class 2 work in NSW is regulated under the Design and Building Practitioners scheme. Registered practitioners must prepare and declare regulated designs, lodge them before work, and lodge as-built declarations before occupation. See the NSW regulator's [Class 2 building obligations](https://www.nsw.gov.au/housing-and-construction/compliance-and-regulation/professionals-working-on-regulated-buildings/design-and-building-practitioners/building-obligations).
- Every new residence needs BASIX commitments for water, energy and thermal performance. The [current BASIX standards](https://www.planningportal.nsw.gov.au/BASIX-standards) include an average 7-star NatHERS target and a 6-star minimum for each apartment, subject to the current application/transitional rules.
- Accessibility does not translate to “every small block must have a lift”, but the common-area accessible path rules are design-critical. If a lift or ramp serves a level, the NCC extends accessibility to the dwellings and common areas on the served levels. The precise arrangement must be resolved early with an access consultant and certifier under [NCC Part D4](https://ncc.abcb.gov.au/editions/ncc-2022/adopted/volume-one/d-access-and-egress/part-d4-access-people-disability).
- A Class 2 building with a rise in storeys of four or more and effective height no more than 25 m generally requires sprinklers under [NCC Part E1](https://ncc.abcb.gov.au/editions/ncc-2022/adopted/volume-one/e-services-and-equipment/part-e1-fire-fighting-equipment). Lower buildings still require the applicable fire-resistance, compartmentation, exits, detection and firefighting provisions.

A lift, basement and fourth storey can therefore create a sharp cost step: structure/excavation, waterproofing, ventilation, pumps, fire services, emergency power/interfaces, accessibility and continuing owners-corporation maintenance all increase together.

## Indicative project budget

### Assumptions

These budgets assume a flat, normally accessible Sydney site; ordinary mid-market finishes; surface or undercroft parking; no land-value uplift; friends retain the homes; and no commercial developer profit or sales/marketing allowance. They are gross cash budgets with GST allowed where normally payable, but the actual GST treatment and input-tax recovery depend on the legal/tax structure. Finance assumes roughly 18–30 months from purchase through completion and is especially sensitive to equity and lender terms.

Current published apartment benchmarks are broad. Rider Levett Bucknall's official [2026 Sydney Rider's Digest](https://www.rlb.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/1/2026/01/2026-RLB-Rider-Digest_Sydney_Digital_2.pdf) gives Q4 2025 benchmarks of A$350,000–675,000 per 85–120 m² walk-up unit and A$475,000–600,000 per 90–120 m² unit in a lift-served building up to ten storeys. Those figures exclude GST, land, parking and site works. It separately benchmarks undercroft parking at A$27,250–48,750 per car and non-CBD basement parking at A$55,000–100,000 per car. A tiny bespoke all-three-bedroom project loses the economies of a larger block, so this budget uses the upper half and adds site works, parking and GST. RLB forecast Sydney escalation of about 4% for 2026 ([RLB escalation outlook](https://www.rlb.com/oceania/insight/construction-cost-escalation-to-remain-elevated-in-2026/)); tendered prices should replace these assumptions before committing to the land.

| Cash item | 4-home, 2-storey scheme | 6-apartment, 3-storey scheme |
|---|---:|---:|
| Land | A$1.00m | A$1.00m |
| Transfer duty, conveyancing and acquisition due diligence | A$0.05–0.08m | A$0.05–0.08m |
| Demolition, hard construction, parking/site works and GST | A$2.60–4.10m | A$3.80–6.10m |
| Design and professional team | A$0.26–0.62m | A$0.38–0.92m |
| Approvals, contributions, utilities and strata setup | A$0.11–0.33m | A$0.16–0.43m |
| Design/construction contingency | A$0.26–0.62m | A$0.38–0.92m |
| Interest, lender costs, rates/insurance and holding | A$0.20–0.60m | A$0.30–0.90m |
| **Indicative total** | **A$4.5–7.3m** | **A$6.1–10.3m** |

The 2024 NSW Productivity-commissioned [CIE feasibility study](https://www.productivity.nsw.gov.au/sites/default/files/2024-11/2024114_CIE-report-Cost-and-feasibility-estimates-for-supplying-residential-dwellings.pdf.pdf) is a useful cross-check: its modelled Sydney 4–9-storey apartment averaged about A$905,000 per dwelling in 2023 including land, finance, taxes and an 18% developer margin. It represents larger, more repeatable developments and a typical—not all three-bedroom—mix, so a six-unit bespoke block can reasonably cost more per home even without a developer margin.

One easily missed cost is Home Building Compensation Fund cover. A qualifying new Class 2 residential building of three storeys or fewer can require HBCF cover; icare's [current premium schedule](https://preview.icare.nsw.gov.au/builders-and-homeowners/builders-and-distributors/premiums/premium-rates) lists a 13.503% Sydney base rate for new apartment-building construction before builder-specific adjustments and broker charges. Ask every builder explicitly whether the tender includes it: an exclusion could mean hundreds of thousands of dollars. For taller buildings outside HBCF, the [Strata Building Bond and Inspections Scheme](https://www.nsw.gov.au/housing-and-construction/building/strata-building-bond-and-inspections-scheme) generally requires a refundable bond, currently 2% of contract value. The exact storey count and insurance regime must be confirmed by the certifier, lawyer and insurer.

### Contributions and purchase tax

- At the 2026–27 NSW transfer-duty scale, duty on a A$1 million residential purchase is about **A$39,187**, before legal/search costs and any foreign-person surcharge. See [Revenue NSW's current calculator and rates](https://www.revenue.nsw.gov.au/taxes-duties-levies-royalties/transfer-duty/understanding-transfer-duty/calculate-transfer-duty).
- Council may levy a local **s7.11** per-dwelling/infrastructure contribution or a **s7.12** percentage-of-cost levy under its local plan. Councils generally recognise existing-development demand for s7.11, but the precise credit is plan-specific; see the state's [existing development credit guidance](https://www.planningportal.nsw.gov.au/local-infrastructure-contributions/credits-existing-development-past-contributions?pm=Section+7-11+contributions+practice+note).
- The separate Greater Sydney **Housing and Productivity Contribution** is indexed. From 1 July 2026 the medium/high-density rate is about **A$10,833.94 per dwelling** ([current state rates](https://www.planning.nsw.gov.au/policy-and-legislation/infrastructure/infrastructure-funding-policies/housing-and-productivity-contribution)). On a four- or six-dwelling development, that is roughly A$43,336 or A$65,004. The state's [worked examples](https://www.planningportal.nsw.gov.au/sites/default/files/documents/2024/HPC%20Worked%20Examples_0.pdf) indicate that demolishing an existing house does not earn a one-dwelling credit for this contribution.

## Approval path

1. **Before exchanging contracts:** obtain title/easements, survey and a current s10.7 planning certificate; inspect the address in the [NSW Planning Spatial Viewer](https://www.planningportal.nsw.gov.au/spatialviewer/); verify the controlling LEP/DCP and state maps; get sewer/stormwater/service information; and commission preliminary planning, architecture, arborist, geotechnical, contamination/asbestos and flood/bushfire advice as relevant.
2. **Concept test:** have a Sydney town planner and registered architect draw a code-compliant test fit with 4- and 6-dwelling options. Ask council for pre-DA advice if using the DA route. Obtain a quantity surveyor's elemental estimate and a lender term sheet before the purchase becomes unconditional.
3. **Approval:** use a CDC only if a qualifying manor-house design and the site meet every Code standard; otherwise lodge a DA through [NSW Online DA](https://www.planningportal.nsw.gov.au/onlineDA). A conventional RFB normally requires a DA. Pattern Book designs have a separate [fast-track pathway](https://www.planning.nsw.gov.au/government-architect-nsw/housing-design/nsw-housing-pattern-book/fast-track-your-planning-approval), but still require an eligible site and all applicable approvals.
4. **After consent:** obtain the construction certificate, lodge the required Class 2 designs/declarations, demolish safely, build, inspect and obtain the occupation certificate. The Planning Portal summarises the [post-consent certificates](https://www.planningportal.nsw.gov.au/development-and-assessment/post-consent-certificates).

Do not demolish merely because demolition itself qualifies as complying development. Preserve the existing home until the replacement project is approved and funded. SafeWork NSW requires asbestos and demolition hazards to be addressed; see its [demolition guidance](https://www.safework.nsw.gov.au/your-industry/construction/demolition).

## How the friends should own it

There are two broad models:

- **Strata subdivision:** each household ultimately owns a separate lot plus a share of common property. Registration creates individual title folios and an owners corporation; see the NSW Land Registry's [strata registration guidance](https://rg-guidelines.nswlrs.com.au/strata_schemes/registration_of_the_strata_plan/). This is usually cleaner for individual mortgages and later exits, but requires subdivision consent/certification, a surveyor, unit entitlements, by-laws, insurance and ongoing owners-corporation governance.
- **One title held as tenants in common:** each friend owns a percentage of the whole property, not legal title to “their apartment”. It can avoid immediate subdivision but often makes finance, departure, default and death much harder.

Whichever route is intended must be decided **before buying**, because changing interests later can create transfer duty, capital-gains tax and GST consequences. A property lawyer should prepare a co-ownership/development agreement covering deposits, cost overruns and capital calls, decision thresholds, exclusive occupation, builder disputes, insurance, default, death/disability, relationship breakdown, valuation, sale/exit rights and what happens if approval only yields four rather than six homes. Each friend should receive independent advice. A tax adviser should also determine whether the group is merely co-owning homes or conducting a property-development enterprise.

Foreign-person status is a potential deal-breaker. Australia currently generally prohibits foreign persons from buying established dwellings until 30 June 2029, subject to limited exceptions ([Australian Government guidance](https://foreigninvestment.gov.au/guidance/types-investments/residential-land)); the ordinary redevelopment exception generally expects at least 20 additional dwellings, not four or six ([ATO foreign-investor guidance](https://www.ato.gov.au/individuals-and-families/investments-and-assets/foreign-resident-investments/foreign-investment-in-australia/residential-property-application-for-foreign-investors)). NSW foreign-person surcharge purchaser duty and land tax may also apply.

## Practical go/no-go brief for a buyer's agent

Do not shortlist a property unless a planner can provisionally confirm all of the following:

- the exact zoning and mapped LMR/TOD status permit the intended form;
- the lot has enough area **and frontage**, preferably about 625 m² / 15 m for a four-home manor concept and materially more for six large apartments at low FSR;
- heritage, flooding, bushfire, aircraft noise, biodiversity, contamination and easements are manageable;
- a plausible envelope fits the FSR, height, setbacks, ADG/DCP open space, deep soil, trees, privacy and solar requirements;
- cars can enter, turn and park without an uneconomic basement, and waste/fire-service access works;
- sewer, water, power and stormwater have adequate capacity;
- a registered architect, town planner and quantity surveyor have reviewed the concept before the contract becomes unconditional; and
- the group has a binding ownership, finance, cost-overrun and exit agreement.

**Bottom line:** on the assumed A$1 million site, four homes may be feasible as a deliberately simple two-storey manor-house project at roughly A$4.5–7.3 million all-in. Six full three-bedroom apartments are a different scale: expect roughly A$6.1–10.3 million and require either substantially more land at low FSR or a correctly mapped higher-density/TOD site. The zoning map and an architect's test fit—not the purchase price—decide whether the idea survives first contact with the rules.

> This is an early feasibility study, not planning, legal, building, tax or financial advice. Controls, contribution rates and construction prices change; verify them for the exact address and application date.
